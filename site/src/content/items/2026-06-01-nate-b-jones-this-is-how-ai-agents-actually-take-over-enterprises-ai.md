## Key Points

- AI agents initially resemble a talented new hire who can read the company wiki.
- Within a few months, agents have processed hundreds of code reviews and architectural discussions.
- Agents synthesize knowledge across organizational silos that usually remain disconnected in human workflows.
- By around six months, agents know composite things that no single human in the company knows.
- Mature installations form a network of agents that act as the enterprise’s institutional knowledge layer.
- Agents can become productive within days, much faster than new engineers can onboard.
- These agents can accelerate human onboarding by directing new employees’ work across the enterprise.
- Agent learning and value accrual may happen in days rather than months as models become more capable.

## Notes

## Initial Phase: Generic but Capable Agents

At the beginning of deployment, the AI agents in an enterprise are described as "smart but generic." They are compared to a talented new hire who can read the company wiki and understand existing documentation. At this stage, their value is real but limited to what they can infer from static knowledge sources and general capabilities. They are not yet deeply specialized to the company’s unique history, decisions, and cross-team context.

## Compounding Learning Over Months

By around month three, the agents have consumed far more than just the wiki. They have processed hundreds of code reviews and architectural discussions. This exposure lets them internalize technical decisions, patterns, and trade-offs made across the organization. Crucially, they synthesize information across silos, combining context from different teams that would normally remain fragmented. This cross-silo synthesis represents a step-change in value because humans rarely see all these streams together in real time.

## Emergence of Super-Human Organizational Knowledge

By about month six, the agents possess integrated knowledge that no single person in the company holds. They connect decisions across teams and projects that would never surface in normal human workflows. This is not just more information, but new composites of understanding created by linking disparate sources. The speaker emphasizes that agents probably learn even faster than this timeline suggests. The implied trajectory is that the agents’ knowledge becomes both broader and more interconnected than any individual’s understanding.

## Mature Installation: Institutional Knowledge Layer

Once this learning compounds, a mature installation looks like a network of agents embedded throughout the enterprise. This network functions as the institutional knowledge layer of the company. Instead of institutional knowledge being scattered in people’s heads, documents, and chats, the agents collectively hold and operationalize it. They are able to recall, relate, and apply past decisions and context across the entire organization. The timing to reach this state can shrink from months to just days as underlying models grow more capable.

## Speed vs. Humans: Onboarding and Coordination

New human engineers might need weeks to onboard effectively into the company. In contrast, agents can be up and productive in a few days once connected to the relevant data and systems. Beyond their own productivity, the agents can accelerate human onboarding. They can direct work for humans across teams, guiding what to do and where to look based on the institutional context they hold. From the moment they are mature enough, these agents are not just helpers but coordinators, influencing how work is organized and executed across the entire enterprise.
