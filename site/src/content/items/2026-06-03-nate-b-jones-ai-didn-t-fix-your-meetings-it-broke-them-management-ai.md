## Key Points

- Correctness encompasses architectural soundness, strategic coherence, customer fit, polish, and freedom from subtle compounding errors.
- True correctness is scarce, especially when subtle errors can pass demos yet fail in production.
- A 2025 Harvard Business School field experiment examined real innovation challenges at Procter & Gamble.
- The study included 776 professionals working on authentic company innovation problems.
- Teams that used AI in this experiment significantly improved their idea quality.
- AI-using teams were three times more likely to generate ideas in the top 10% of quality.
- This threefold improvement concerns quality and correctness, not simply more ideas or higher output volume.
- The key benefit observed is being right at the highest level, not just producing more work.

## Notes

## Scarcity of Correctness

The speaker defines correctness as more than just basic accuracy. It includes whether a shipped solution is architecturally sound, strategically coherent, right for the customer, polished in its execution, and free of subtle errors. These subtle errors are particularly dangerous because they can appear fine in a demo environment yet compound into real failures in production settings. Within this framing, correctness in its full sense is described as scarce, implying that many outputs look acceptable superficially but fail on deeper robustness and long-term reliability.

## Dimensions of Being “Right”

Being right involves multiple interconnected dimensions. Architecturally sound means the underlying structure of the solution is solid and maintainable. Strategically coherent means the work aligns with broader goals and direction. Being right for the customer means it truly addresses user needs and context. Polished refers to the level of refinement and lack of rough edges in the final product. Freedom from subtle errors means avoiding small, initially invisible flaws that only emerge as serious issues under real-world use.

## The Harvard Business School Field Experiment

A Harvard Business School field experiment published in 2025 is used as direct evidence about AI’s impact. The researchers studied 776 professionals at Procter & Gamble, focusing on real innovation challenges rather than artificial test tasks. These professionals worked on authentic company problems, giving the results practical relevance. The experiment explicitly evaluated how AI affected outcomes in this genuine innovation context.

## AI’s Effect on Idea Quality

In this experiment, teams that used AI showed a striking difference in the quality of their ideas. AI-using teams were three times more likely to produce ideas that ranked in the top 10% of quality. This top decile benchmark suggests the study focused on the highest standards of performance. Importantly, the improvement is not framed as simply generating more ideas. The emphasis is that teams became three times more likely to be right at the highest level. The distinction is between quantity of output and depth of correctness.

## Quality Versus Quantity of Output

The speaker underscores that the result is not “three times more output.” Instead, the measured effect is heavily skewed toward correctness at the top tier of performance. This suggests that AI’s value in the study lay in raising the probability that a team’s best ideas would truly be excellent. It ties back to the initial idea that correctness—deep, multidimensional rightness—is scarce. Within that scarcity, AI appears to substantially increase the odds of producing those rare, truly high-quality ideas.
