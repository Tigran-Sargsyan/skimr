## Key Points

- Traditional platforms like Salesforce mainly lock in customers through their data.
- AI context platforms will lock in customers through synthesized understanding, not just stored data.
- The agent in a context platform understands relationships between systems like Salesforce, GitHub, and board materials.
- This cross-system understanding cannot be exported in a usable way to another platform.
- Raw enterprise data is ultimately portable between systems, even if inconvenient.
- Synthesized organizational knowledge accumulated over a year is effectively non-portable.
- This creates a new kind of lock-in described as comprehension or intelligence lock-in.
- Comprehension lock-in will compound daily, turning the context platform into a powerful flywheel.

## Notes

## From Data Lock-In to Understanding Lock-In

The speaker contrasts traditional enterprise platforms like Salesforce with emerging AI-driven context platforms. In the traditional model, the primary source of lock-in is the data itself: customer records, activity histories, and other stored information. While there may be friction, this data is, in principle, portable and can be migrated to another system.

In the new model, the critical asset is not just the data but the *synthesis layer* that sits across the entire stack. This layer is embodied by an agent that understands how Salesforce data connects to GitHub activity, and how both relate to artifacts like the board deck. It represents an integrated, cross-system comprehension of the organization’s workflows, decisions, and history.

## Non-Portable Synthesis vs. Portable Data

The speaker emphasizes that this understanding "can’t be exported." Moving away from a context platform does not just mean moving tables or files; it means abandoning a learned, integrated model of how everything relates. This is described as fundamentally different from a simple model-choice conversation or an infrastructure swap.

Data itself is considered "ultimately portable" because it can be exported, transformed, and imported elsewhere with sufficient effort. In contrast, a year’s worth of synthesized organizational knowledge, captured and refined by an AI context platform, is effectively non-portable. Rebuilding that level of understanding in a new system would be massively costly or practically impossible.

## Comprehension / Intelligence Lock-In

To describe this phenomenon, the speaker introduces terms like "comprehension lock-in" and "intelligence lock-in." These labels underscore that the binding force is the system’s accumulated grasp of the organization: its patterns, relationships, and context, not merely its stored data.

This form of lock-in is described as the deepest that has ever existed in enterprise software. It goes beyond configuration complexity or proprietary formats, reaching into the unique, emergent intelligence that develops as the system observes and connects everything over time.

## Compounding Effects and the Flywheel

The lock-in effect is not static; it compounds with every day the platform operates. Each interaction, decision, and data point further refines the platform’s understanding, making it more valuable and harder to leave.

The speaker asks the listener to "fast forward" and imagine how this accumulated comprehension becomes a flywheel. As understanding deepens, the platform becomes more useful, which drives greater usage, which in turn accelerates the growth of synthesized knowledge. This compounding loop strengthens comprehension lock-in, making the context platform an increasingly central and irreplaceable part of the enterprise stack.
