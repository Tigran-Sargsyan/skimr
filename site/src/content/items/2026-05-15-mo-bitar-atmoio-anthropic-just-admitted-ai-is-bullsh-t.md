## Key Points

- Forward deployed engineers are elite contractors embedded at client companies to build AI-powered prototypes.
- Anthropic and OpenAI are investing billions to scale “humans as a service” for AI deployment in enterprises.
- These engineers build fast, impressive demos that often degrade into “slop” once real users and scale arrive.
- The creator claims AI tools have made many engineers lazier and more dependent, reducing intrinsic motivation to code.
- He argues labs promised autonomous labor-replacing AI but now profit by renting out human experts instead.
- A dystopian scenario is proposed where Anthropic and OpenAI monopolize access to high-end software-building models.
- The speaker suggests job seekers copy Anthropic’s forward deployed engineer responsibilities into their resumes to pass AI screeners.
- He recommends using AI itself during interviews and technical tasks, framing effective tool use as the new core skill.

## Notes

## The “Forward Deployed Engineer” Phenomenon

- The video centers on Anthropic’s new role: “Forward Deployed Engineer,” which the creator calls one of the most aggressive, impressive job titles in tech.
- The term comes from Palantir, where engineers are “deployed” into client companies to build and adapt software within their environments.
- Anthropic has launched a $1.5B joint venture with Blackstone and Goldman Sachs to place such engineers into enterprises.
- OpenAI responded with its own initiative, DeployCo, a $14B venture, similarly focused on embedding human experts to implement AI.
- The creator characterizes this as “humans as a service,” noting the irony that AI labs meant to automate knowledge work are effectively building temp agencies for highly trained engineers.

## What Forward Deployed Engineers Actually Do

- These engineers are portrayed as “Navy SEALs of AI,” highly polished, elite specialists who drop into companies and rapidly build impressive systems.
- Their core function is to create AI-powered demos and prototypes extremely quickly, leveraging tools like Claude.
- The demo systems look amazing to non-technical executives: fast turnaround, detailed behavior, slick automation.
- However, the creator insists that underneath the shine, it is often “slop”: brittle, poorly engineered systems that don’t hold up under real-world usage.
- Initially, with few users and limited scale, these systems appear to work well, reinforcing the sense of breakthrough productivity.
- Over months, as users and complexity grow, problems surface and the underlying “slop” becomes evident—just as the original forward deployed engineers have already left.
- When issues emerge, companies are sold more humans: new forward deployed engineers, not necessarily the original team, to “fix” or rebuild what was left.

## The Implied Confession About AI’s Limits

- The creator interprets these roles as an admission by AI labs that their products are not autonomous replacements for human labor.
- Labs originally marketed AI as something companies could “plug in” to replace or automate large portions of white-collar work, run directly by executives.
- In reality, he argues, effective deployment requires skilled humans deeply embedded in customer systems, stitching models into fragile workflows.
- Thus, the real product is not disembodied AI, but packaged human expertise plus AI, sold as high-end consulting.
- He calls this “the greatest con in the history of capitalism”: AI labs spread tools that make in-house engineers dependent and “lazy,” then sell elite contractors as the antidote.

## Dystopian Software Future: Centralized Model Gatekeepers

- The creator sketches a dystopian trajectory: in 10 years, most companies no longer employ software engineers directly.
- Instead, firms that need software go to Anthropic or OpenAI, who maintain massive warehouses of forward deployed engineers.
- These internal armies handle all serious software development, leveraging privileged access to the best models at cost.
- In this world, virtually all meaningful software is built under the control of a few labs; normal companies just order what they need.
- A key assumption: people will not have open access to the truly capable, software-building models.
- He claims Anthropic’s Mythos initiative exemplifies this: consumer models will help with cooking, poetry, and health advice, but high-end software models will be tightly controlled.
- Over time, Anthropic could become a “hundred trillion dollar company,” gatekeeping advanced software creation and security.

## Role of Model Access and Open Source

- The creator argues that a healthier, less dystopian future depends on broad, fair access to powerful models.
- If Anthropic and OpenAI keep models open enough, and open-source models keep pace, individuals and companies will retain the ability to build their own software.
- However, he suggests there will always be at least one model held back by Anthropic—especially for security-sensitive tasks.
- In one scenario, labs might give access to a strong software model but with many security vulnerabilities, making it hard to deploy safely.
- Open-source and mid-tier models could still support “mid-grade” in-house software, enough for “cute” applications but not matching Mythos-level quality.

## Strategy for Workers: Rebranding as Forward Deployed Talent

- The creator pivots to practical advice: how to position oneself in this shifting labor market.
- Anthropic’s forward deployed engineer job description becomes a template for an ideal resume in the AI era.
- He instructs viewers to open the job posting, copy the responsibilities section, and adapt the wording into their own resumes.
- Example responsibilities include working inside customer systems with Claude models, building production applications, delivering technical artifacts like MCP servers and sub-agents, and staying current on LLM capabilities.
- The idea: AI screeners at companies are likely using Anthropic-aligned criteria, so mirroring this language optimizes for automated resume filters.
- Since many hiring workflows involve AI matching job descriptions to resumes, he claims this alignment practically guarantees callbacks.

## Interview Prep: Become a “Mini-Daario”

- To handle interviews, the creator points to a popular AI interviewer (Daario) as a model of preparation.
- A New York Times profile, he says, revealed that Daario’s apparent omniscience comes from intense preparation: flashcards, personal tutors, and continuous study.
- Viewers are urged to emulate this by deeply learning AI concepts, architectures, and patterns at a theoretical level, even without building much.
- These topics—agentic paradigms, orchestration, multi-agent management—become the new equivalent of studying algorithms for LeetCode.
- Candidates should have rehearsed answers to questions like managing 100 agents or throughput of pull requests per day.

## Using AI During Technical Interviews

- For technical screens, the creator suggests that if you can already code, the new bar is being able to orchestrate AI effectively.
- Typical prompts might involve building multi-agent orchestration systems or complex AI workflows, aligned with the buzzwords on the resume.
- His advice: during these tasks, openly or covertly use Claude to do most of the heavy lifting.
- You “brain dump” design ideas, constraints, and goals into Claude, then copy and adapt what it produces.
- The human’s apparent contribution is high, but in practice most of the work is performed by the model.
- He insists this is not cheating but is precisely what “AI engineering” now means: skillfully steering models to produce solutions.
- Interviewers see a candidate producing complex systems in real time and attribute the performance to the candidate’s expertise.

## Leveraging the System: Self-Deployment as a Strategy

- The creator references Soham, a notorious figure who held many remote jobs simultaneously by mastering interviews and then minimizing actual work.
- He suggests that once hired, workers can similarly negotiate the realities of AI-driven productivity and slack, just as Soham did with traditional roles.
- The overarching message is to “forward deploy yourself”: adopt the branding, clothing, and demeanor that signal modern AI fluency (e.g., turtlenecks, half-sleeve shirts, distinctive style).
- Ultimately, he sees the near-term winning move for individuals as rebranding around forward deployed responsibilities, mastering AI-tools, and using the system’s own automation to advance their careers.
