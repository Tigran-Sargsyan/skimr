## Key Points

- Overly pragmatic, top‑down approaches to coding agents and AI use cases often lead to failure.
- Approved AI use cases differ across teams and functions within the same company.
- Teams need freedom to discover which AI workflows actually work in their local context.
- Successful AI “recipes” should not remain isolated within individual pockets of the company.
- Organizations must intentionally create forums where effective AI patterns can be shared quickly.
- Rapid exchange of proven AI workflows helps them spread across the company.
- AI adoption is more effective when it emerges from real use rather than rigid initial planning.

## Notes

## Failure of Strict Top‑Down AI Adoption
The speaker highlights a recurring failure pattern in organizational AI adoption. Companies often try a pragmatic but rigid, top‑down approach, defining in advance which AI agents are allowed and what the “approved” use cases should be. While this might seem orderly and low‑risk, it tends to break down in practice. The core issue is that AI’s usefulness is highly context‑dependent, so centrally defining everything upfront misses many real opportunities and misfits.

## Context‑Specific Use Cases
Different parts of a company use information, processes, and tools in very different ways. As a result, the AI use cases that actually work will vary significantly from one team or function to another. A generic, company‑wide list of “correct” agent behaviors or workflows cannot capture these nuances. For AI to be genuinely useful, each pocket of the organization needs room to explore how AI best supports its specific work.

## Allowing Local Discovery
The speaker emphasizes the importance of letting people locally discover what works with AI. Instead of prescribing every use case, leaders should enable experimentation within teams and roles. This grassroots discovery lets employees find concrete, high‑value workflows that fit their real daily tasks. Those emergent solutions are often more practical and effective than centrally designed plans.

## Spreading Effective “Recipes”
Once useful patterns or “recipes” for using AI emerge within a team, they should not stay isolated. The organization’s job is then to ensure these successful practices can spread quickly to other relevant areas. The speaker suggests creating the right forums or mechanisms where people can share how they are using agents effectively.

## Creating Forums for Knowledge Exchange
These forums might take the form of internal communities, show‑and‑tell sessions, documentation hubs, or other sharing mechanisms, though the speaker does not specify exact formats. The essential idea is deliberate design of spaces where AI usage knowledge can move rapidly between teams. Through such forums, effective recipes propagate across the company, improving overall AI adoption without relying on rigid, top‑down prescriptions.
