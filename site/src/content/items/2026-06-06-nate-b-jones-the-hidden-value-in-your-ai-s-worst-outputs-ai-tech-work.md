## Key Points

- There is currently no real infrastructure for scaling notes in the age of AI.
- The absence of scalable note systems is described as the largest structural gap in the AI tool ecosystem.
- Organizations using AI generate huge numbers of rejections at the individual user level.
- Almost all user rejections of AI outputs are effectively discarded instead of being captured.
- The speaker argues that spreadsheets, databases, and dashboards are the wrong tools for capturing this rejection data.
- People will not reliably context switch to a separate tool just to capture feedback or notes.
- The capture of feedback must occur directly where work happens, inside the AI conversation itself.
- Rejection capture should be a side effect of the rejection users are already performing, not an extra task.

## Notes

## The Missing Infrastructure for AI-Era Notes

The speaker argues that the necessary infrastructure to fully exploit AI in everyday work is still missing. Specifically, they highlight that no one is seriously addressing how to scale notes in the AI era. This is framed not as a minor tooling issue but as a central structural gap in the broader AI tools ecosystem.

In their view, the gap centers on how organizations handle the continuous stream of AI outputs that users reject.

## Rejections as Wasted Signal

Every organization using AI systems generates a large volume of rejections at the grassroots level, at the individual user’s seat. Each time a user discards an AI output, that action contains implicit information about quality, relevance, or context. The speaker claims that, almost without exception, this data is simply falling on the floor.

These discarded rejections represent a hidden layer of value and learning that is currently not being captured or reused.

## Why Existing Tools Fail

The speaker rejects the idea that the solution lies in conventional tools like spreadsheets, databases, or dashboards. They argue that any approach requiring users to leave their current workflow and context switch is doomed to low adoption. Users will not reliably move to a separate system just to log feedback, notes, or rejections.

Because of this, the typical “log it somewhere else” mindset is seen as fundamentally misaligned with real user behavior.

## Capture Must Happen Where Work Happens

The proposed principle is that capture must occur exactly where work is done. For AI tools, this is inside the conversation interface itself. Feedback and rejection data should be collected as a natural part of the interaction, not as an extra chore.

The speaker believes capture has to happen inside the AI conversation as a side effect of the rejection the user is already performing. In other words, turning the act of rejecting a bad output into an automatic, structured capture event is the key to scaling notes and closing this structural gap.
