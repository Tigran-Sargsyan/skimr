## Key Points

- Learning AI in the abstract is now basic table stakes rather than a major advantage.
- The critical question is whether you operate on the AI capability frontier or on a flat, comfortable curve.
- Frontier users regularly test new models and integrate them into real, domain-specific workflows.
- Frontier users build evaluation frameworks tailored to their domain to judge AI performance and reliability.
- Comfortable users are aware of AI but largely work as they did two years ago.
- Economic value in the next few years will concentrate in the gap between frontier and comfortable users.
- Social inertia will slow the closing of this capability gap more than many people expect.
- Domain-specific AI fluency becomes a compounding asset, growing more valuable as models gain new capabilities.

## Notes

## Frontier vs. Comfortable: Mapping Your Position

The core idea is to “map the capability dissipation gap” for yourself, meaning you should honestly assess where you sit between the AI capability frontier and a more static, comfortable mode of working.

The speaker stresses that simply “learning AI” at a generic level was 2024 advice and now counts as table stakes. Knowing about AI in the abstract or dabbling with basic tools is no longer sufficient to create significant differentiation or value.

Instead, the crucial question is your position relative to two curves:
- An exponential capability curve, where AI systems are rapidly improving.
- A flat curve, where work habits and workflows remain mostly unchanged.

## Characteristics of Frontier Users

Operating at the “capability frontier” involves several concrete behaviors:
- Regularly testing new AI models as they are released.
- Integrating AI into real, day-to-day workflows rather than limiting it to experiments or side projects.
- Building evaluation frameworks specific to your domain so you can systematically judge whether AI outputs are good enough, where they fail, and how to rely on them.

This kind of engagement creates “genuine AI fluency” within a specific context, not just surface-level familiarity. It requires time working directly with models, iterating, and refining how they are used for real tasks.

## Characteristics of Comfortable Users

On the other side are people who are aware AI exists and may occasionally use it, but whose fundamental ways of working look almost the same as two years ago.

They may be content with the current “dissipation rate” of AI capabilities into their workflows—that is, they allow advances to seep in slowly, if at all, instead of actively pursuing them. Their workflows remain mostly flat while the capability curve rises.

## Where Economic Value Concentrates

The “gap” between frontier users and comfortable users is where the speaker claims economic value will concentrate over the next two to three years.

Because the AI capability curve is steep and many people are slow to meaningfully change how they work, that gap represents a widening performance and value differential between these two groups.

Social inertia—habits, organizational resistance, and personal reluctance to change—means this gap will not close as quickly as many might assume. People will often stay in comfortable, familiar workflows even as AI capabilities accelerate.

## AI Fluency as a Compounding Asset

For those who have spent the past year building real AI fluency in their domain, the payoff is more than just knowing a new tool.

Their fluency becomes an asset that compounds over time. Each new model improvement arrives on top of an existing foundation of understanding: they already know how to probe models, integrate them into processes, and evaluate outputs in context.

As a result, every incremental capability increase makes their existing asset—workflow design, prompts, evaluation methods, intuition about failure modes—more valuable, not less. The time required to develop this kind of practical, domain-specific understanding cannot be shortcut, giving those who started early and operate at the frontier a durable advantage as AI continues to advance.
