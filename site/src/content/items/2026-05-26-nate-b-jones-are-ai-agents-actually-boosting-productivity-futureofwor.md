## Key Points

- US productivity reportedly grew about 2.7% in 2025, roughly double the decade average.
- The author Eric Björnsson attributes a meaningful portion of this productivity growth to AI and AI agents.
- AI adoption is uneven, and many users only talk to single chatbots without changing workflows.
- Simply prompting a chatbot occasionally does not count as real AI adoption in this framework.
- Top performers are restructuring their work so AI functions as a primary collaborator in their processes.
- Effective collaboration requires AI systems to retain memory about the user and their ongoing context.
- In one workflow, the user must repeatedly explain their role, project, constraints, and decisions to the AI.
- In a better workflow, tools like MCP server in Open Bridge let the AI pre-know the user’s role, projects, constraints, team, and recent decisions.

## Notes

## Context: Productivity Growth and AI
- The speaker references an article by Eric Björnsson in the Financial Times, claiming US productivity grew about 2.7% in 2025.
- This 2.7% figure is presented as roughly double the average productivity growth over the prior decade.
- Björnsson is said to assign a significant portion of this improvement to the use of AI and AI agents, suggesting a macro-level impact of these tools on economic productivity.

## Uneven AI Adoption
- The speaker stresses that AI adoption is not uniform across workers or organizations.
- Many people only “use AI” by occasionally talking to a single chatbot.
- This kind of superficial engagement is argued not to constitute genuine AI adoption.
- Real adoption, in this framing, requires rethinking and redesigning workflows around AI capabilities.

## Redefining Workflows with AI as a Primary Collaborator
- The people achieving outsized productivity results are described as those who restructure how they work around AI.
- They are not merely waiting for better models; instead, they focus on deeper integration of current models into daily operations.
- AI is positioned as a “primary collaborator,” meaning it participates continuously in tasks, decisions, and projects rather than acting as an occasional query tool.

## The Importance of Memory and Context
- The speaker argues that true collaboration with AI is impossible if the system has no memory of the user.
- A stateless chatbot that forgets past interactions forces the user to re-supply the same information repeatedly.
- Persistent memory of the user’s role, projects, constraints, and prior decisions is framed as essential for AI to add real leverage.

## Workflow A: Stateless Chatbot Interaction
- In the first example, Person A opens Claude with no prior context stored.
- They must spend about four minutes explaining their role, current project, constraints, and the decision they are trying to make.
- After this briefing, they get a good answer, but only after this upfront cost of re-establishing context.
- This illustrates a common, inefficient pattern where each AI interaction starts from scratch.

## Workflow B: Context-Rich, Integrated AI Agent
- In the contrasting example, Person B opens Claude within an integrated environment.
- Claude already knows her role, active projects, constraints, team members, and decisions made the previous week.
- This persistent knowledge is said to be maintained via an MCP server within a system called Open Bridge.
- Because the AI starts from an informed baseline, Person B avoids repeatedly re-explaining context and can move directly to higher-value decision support.

## Core Claim About Productivity Gains
- The speaker’s central claim is that the major productivity boosts come from scenarios like Person B’s workflow.
- Tools and setups that give AI ongoing memory and integration with work systems enable it to function as a true collaborator.
- Superficial chatbot use, as in Person A’s scenario, may provide some benefit but will not match the productivity gains attributed to serious AI adoption in the productivity statistics.
