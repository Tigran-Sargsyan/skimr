## Key Points

- Standard models place Neanderthals and Denisovans as sister groups that diverged from modern humans 700–800,000 years ago.
- Genetic evidence indicates interbreeding 200–300,000 years ago, with modern humans contributing about 5% of Neanderthal ancestry.
- Archaeologically, Neanderthals resemble modern humans more than they resemble Denisovans, especially in stone tool technology.
- Middle Stone Age or Middle Paleolithic Levallois technology appears 300–400,000 years ago in regions like the Caucasus and East Africa.
- Mitochondrial DNA and Y chromosomes cluster Neanderthals with modern humans, but nuclear genomes cluster Neanderthals with Denisovans.
- Reich proposes a Middle Stone Age population spread into Europe, mixing with local archaics and becoming mostly archaic genetically while retaining modern culture.
- Simulation work suggests expanding populations can acquire extensive local DNA through introgression while preserving a small founding fraction.
- Reich argues both Neanderthals and modern humans may descend from a key 200–300,000-year-old population that spread its technology and genes into Europe and Africa respectively.

## Notes

## Standard model of human–archaic relationships

- The prevailing view holds that Neanderthals and Denisovans descend from a common ancestral population.
- This Neanderthal–Denisovan branch is thought to have separated from the lineage leading to modern humans roughly 700–800,000 years ago.
- Later, Neanderthals and Denisovans split from one another around 500–600,000 years ago.
- Since 2010, ancient DNA has supported this structure: on average, Denisovans are genetically closer to Neanderthals than to modern humans.

## The puzzling 5% modern-human contribution to Neanderthals

- Genetic studies show an interbreeding event 200–300,000 years ago in which modern-human–related groups contributed DNA to Neanderthal ancestors.
- This event accounts for about 5% of the Neanderthal genome.
- At the same time, archaeological evidence suggests Neanderthals and modern humans are more similar to each other in behavior and tools than either is to Denisovans.
- This tension—archaeological similarity between Neanderthals and modern humans vs. genetic closeness between Neanderthals and Denisovans—motivates Reich to rethink the relationships.

## Middle Stone Age / Middle Paleolithic technological revolution

- A new style of stone tool production, often called the Middle Stone Age in Africa and Middle Paleolithic in Europe, appears around 300–400,000 years ago.
- It involves carefully prepared cores, long-distance transport of high-quality stone (e.g., flint), and more widespread use of fire.
- This technology, also known as Levallois technology, first appears in regions such as the Caucasus (e.g., Georgia) and East Africa.
- Reich notes that this technological package is shared between early modern-human groups in Africa and Neanderthals in Europe, but seems absent in East Asia where Denisovans are found.

## Hypothesis: a key population spreads technology and genes

- Reich is interested in a model where a single key population invents Middle Stone Age / Levallois technology.
- This population is located somewhere around the Near East or adjacent regions and then expands in multiple directions.
- One branch moves into Europe, encounters local archaic humans, and mixes with them.
- The result is a group that becomes genetically mostly local archaic (about 95%) but retains the incoming population’s culture and some genes (about 5%).
- This scenario could naturally correspond to the observed 5% modern-human–related ancestry in Neanderthals.

## Mitochondrial DNA and Y chromosome anomalies

- Whole-genome data cluster Neanderthals with Denisovans, consistent with the standard tree.
- However, mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), inherited maternally, clusters Neanderthals with modern humans instead.
- Similarly, Y chromosome data, inherited paternally, show Neanderthals and modern humans sharing a more recent common ancestor than either shares with Denisovans.
- For Denisovans and modern humans, mtDNA and Y share an ancestor more than 700–800,000 years ago, as expected from the standard divergence.
- For Neanderthals, mtDNA and Y coalesce only 300–450,000 years ago, much more recent than their deep nuclear divergence.

## Why 5% genome but 100% mtDNA and Y is surprising

- Current interpretations assume the ~5% modern-human–related introgression into Neanderthals also replaced Neanderthal mtDNA and Y entirely.
- That would mean a small genomic contribution somehow drove both the maternal and paternal lineages to 100% frequency in Neanderthals.
- Reich argues this is statistically unlikely by chance; with 5% introgression, the probability of fixing both mtDNA and Y independently is roughly 5% × 5%, a very small number.
- Yet this is effectively what the field has come to accept based on existing models.
- The improbability of this dual replacement motivates looking for an alternative demographic explanation.

## Evidence from Sima de los Huesos

- A site in Spain, Sima de los Huesos, dated roughly 300–400,000 years old, provides crucial data.
- Nuclear DNA from individuals at this site is Neanderthal-like, indicating a Neanderthal-lineage population.
- However, their mtDNA and Y chromosomes are Denisovan-like, not modern-human–like.
- This pattern suggests that at some point, a population related to modern humans entered this region and replaced mtDNA and Y while leaving most of the nuclear genome similar to the local archaic group.
- Reich interprets this as strong support for a scenario where an incoming population can swap uniparental lineages while leaving most nuclear ancestry local.

## Wave-front expansion and massive local introgression

- Reich imagines Europe as a landscape of many small demes (local breeding populations), perhaps around a hundred.
- Modern-human–related groups enter near the Middle East and expand westward across this deme-structured landscape.
- Theoretical and simulation studies in various species indicate that when an expanding population encounters resident populations, even low levels of interbreeding can produce massive introgression of local genes into the invaders.
- Pioneers at the wave front often mate with numerous locals, whose DNA is numerically dominant, leading to swamping of the original wave-front genomes.
- By the time the expansion reaches the far side of Europe, the descendants are largely genetically local, even if they originated from a distinct source.

## Cultural retention and matrilineal transmission

- In Reich’s preferred scenario, the invading population brings the Middle Stone Age / Levallois technology and associated cultural traditions.
- These cultural traits, he speculates, could be transmitted matrilineally, for example from mothers to children.
- If so, the mtDNA lineages of the incoming population might be preferentially preserved even as nuclear DNA becomes mostly local.
- The result would be a group that is about 95% local archaic genetically, but retains the newcomers’ mtDNA and culture.
- This could explain why Neanderthals cluster with modern humans in mtDNA and Y, yet show only 5% modern-like nuclear ancestry.

## Parallel story in Africa and mixed origins of modern humans

- Reich notes separate evidence that modern humans themselves are admixed rather than descending from a single “pure” lineage.
- One proposed model is that modern humans are a mixture of two groups that diverged about 1.5 million years ago.
- These two groups come together 200–300,000 years ago, producing a population with roughly 80% ancestry from an early modern lineage and 20% from a deeply archaic African group.
- This admixture event in Africa is roughly contemporaneous with the proposed expansion of the Middle Stone Age population.
- In this model, the same key population that spreads Levallois technology into Europe, forming Neanderthals, also spreads into Africa, contributing heavily to the ancestors of living humans.

## Asymmetry in replacement: Europe vs. Africa

- In Europe, the incoming Middle Stone Age group mixes with local archaics that are only about 700–800,000 years diverged.
- Genetic incompatibilities between the groups are relatively modest, allowing extensive gene flow and resulting in about 95% local ancestry.
- In Africa, the archaic group mixing with early moderns is about 1.5 million years diverged.
- Greater divergence likely created more genetic incompatibilities and stronger barriers to gene flow.
- Thus, in Africa the incoming population is only about 20% replaced by archaic ancestry, preserving more of its genome in the ancestors of modern humans.

## A single formative event shaping Neanderthals and modern humans

- Reich envisions a key “revolutionary” population emerging with Middle Stone Age / Levallois technology around 200–300,000 years ago.
- This population expands into Europe, mixing with local archaics to form Neanderthals that are mostly archaic genetically but culturally modern-human–like.
- It also expands into Africa, mixing with a more divergent archaic group to produce the ancestors of today’s humans with mainly early modern ancestry and a significant archaic component.
- This scenario suggests a shared formative event underlying both Neanderthals and modern humans, beyond the later symbolic and behavioral changes emphasized at 50–100,000 years ago.

## Reframing Neanderthals as close cousins

- If this model is correct, Neanderthals and modern humans share more than a distant common ancestor hundreds of thousands of years ago.
- They would share mtDNA lineages, Y chromosomes, and a common origin in the 200–300,000-year-old Middle Stone Age event.
- They would also share a technological and possibly cultural toolkit centered on Levallois-style stone tools and expanded use of fire.
- In that sense, Neanderthals might be better understood as close cousins within the same broad cultural and demographic transformation that shaped modern humans.
- Reich notes this is a speculative working hypothesis and “probably is wrong,” but he finds it a compelling way to integrate archaeological and genetic anomalies into one coherent picture.
