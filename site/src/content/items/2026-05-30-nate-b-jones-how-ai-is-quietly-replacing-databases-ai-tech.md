## Key Points

- AI context platforms function as the new enterprise data platform rather than a niche product.
- These platforms subsume the value of every connected system of record by owning synthesis across data silos.
- The true value in enterprise data lies in synthesis, not in storage or basic record-keeping.
- Salesforce’s massive valuation comes from owning customer data as a primary system of record today.
- ServiceNow’s valuation similarly rests on controlling IT workflow data as a core system of record.
- A company that controls the synthesis layer across all enterprise data would be worth more than Salesforce and ServiceNow combined.
- Enterprises dislike physically moving data and may choose to keep it in legacy systems like Salesforce.
- If SaaS vendors lose control of synthesis and agentic workflows, their data custody offers little margin and threatens their future.

## Notes

## AI Context Platforms as the New Enterprise Data Platform

The speaker claims that what he calls an "AI context platform" is not just another software product category. Instead, it effectively becomes the new enterprise data platform, encompassing and reshaping the entire enterprise data landscape. Rather than sitting beside existing tools, it is positioned as the central layer that understands, connects, and reasons over all enterprise data.

Crucially, this AI-driven context or synthesis layer is described as subsuming the value of every system of record it connects to. Traditional systems of record—CRMs, IT workflow tools, and other SaaS applications—primarily focus on storing and structuring data. In contrast, the AI context platform derives value from synthesizing that data across different sources, providing higher-order understanding and coordination.

## Where the Economic Value Actually Lives

A central argument is that the economic value in enterprise data has never truly been in storage itself. Instead, it was always in the ability to synthesize data into insights, workflows, and decisions. Data storage is treated as a commodity, while synthesis is the scarce, high-margin capability.

Salesforce’s large valuation is used as an example of how much value has historically accrued to a company that "owns" customer data as a system of record. ServiceNow’s similarly large valuation illustrates the same point for IT workflow data. However, the speaker argues that a company that owns the synthesis layer across all enterprise data—cutting across these individual silos—would be even more valuable than both companies combined.

## Legacy Systems of Record May Persist Physically, But Lose Power

The speaker notes that most enterprises dislike moving data between systems. Because of this, he anticipates a future where many organizations choose to keep their data physically stored in existing systems of record like Salesforce. Data gravity, migration complexity, and organizational inertia mean these legacy platforms may continue to hold the raw records.

However, he stresses that for vendors like Marc Benioff’s Salesforce, merely retaining custody of data is not where the margin and strategic power reside. If an external AI context platform or similar synthesis layer intermediates access to that data, the system of record risks becoming a back-end utility. It would still store information but no longer control how it is interpreted, connected, or acted upon.

## Disintermediation, Agentic Workflows, and SaaS Risk

The speaker warns about the risk of disintermediation for traditional SaaS businesses. Disintermediation here means losing control of the synthesis and workflow layers that sit on top of raw data. If an AI context platform orchestrates reasoning and decision-making, it can become the primary interface through which users and other systems interact with enterprise data.

He emphasizes that agentic workflows—where AI agents autonomously coordinate tasks, decisions, and processes—are part of this synthesis layer. If SaaS vendors are excluded from this agentic and interpretive tier, they effectively forfeit the high-value part of the stack. In that scenario, they remain as storage or record-keeping utilities with limited pricing power and shrinking margins.

From this perspective, the future viability of large SaaS platforms hinges on whether they can participate in, control, or at least tightly integrate with the emerging AI context and agentic workflow layers, rather than simply continuing to serve as the place where data is stored.
